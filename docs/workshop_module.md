# négaWatt-BE — interactive workshop module

*Living document. Started 2026-08-31. Owner: Sylvain Quoilin.*

This file is the single place where the workshop module's **design decisions, open
questions and milestone status** are recorded. If you come back to this in six months
and wonder "why on earth did we do it that way?", the answer should be here.

---

## 1. What this is

The public site (`website/`) shows the scenario's **downstream** hypotheses: 33 read-only
cards for transport, each generated by a notebook. One-way communication.

The workshop module turns that around. Participants — in groups, in a room or remote —
set the **upstream** behavioural and policy assumptions that *produce* those card values
(how many people per car, how much of today's car traffic shifts to other modes, how many
tonnes on the average truck…). They do it **without seeing négaWatt's answer**, helped by
factual anchors. Their values are collected live; a reveal page then shows the group
spread next to négaWatt's value and its written justification, one lever at a time.

Motivation, from the peer review (`docs/review_negaWatt_BE.md` §1.2): *"No uncertainty or
sensitivity analysis. Every parameter is a single deterministic value."* The workshop is a
**participatory sensitivity analysis** — and a communication tool.

Phase 1 covers four topics; only the first is built:

| topic id | Scope | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `inland-mobility` | domestic passenger ground transport + domestic freight | **PoC, in progress** |
| `international-mobility` | intra-EU and extra-EU aviation (+ maritime, absent from the model) | scaffolded only |
| `residential-heat` | residential heating & cooling | scaffolded only |
| `tertiary-heat` | tertiary heating & cooling | scaffolded only |

## 2. Design principles

1. **One lever = one screen**, answerable by a non-expert in about 4 minutes using only
   what is on that screen.
2. **The slider is the 2050 endpoint of the historical curve.** Belgium's real 2000–2023
   trajectory is drawn, and dragging the handle extends it to 2050 as a dashed line. This
   removes the need to explain what is being asked.
3. **Tangible units only.** km per person per day, people per car, tonnes per truck.
   Never `pkm/person/yr` or `% of 2019` in the participant UI.
4. **Ask for an objective, never a forecast.** "What is a realistic 2050 objective for
   car occupancy?" — not "how many people will be in the average car?". The scenario is
   normative, and so is the question; a group asked to predict answers with what it
   expects rather than with what it is prepared to defend.
5. **Four reality anchors per lever**: past evolution · a Belgian structural fact · a
   best-in-class country · a tangible equivalent. The same four are printed on the paper
   cards.
6. **Live leverage.** Moving the slider shows the energy consequence
   ("+33% car energy vs. négaWatt"). This is what turns a guess into an insight.
7. **Anti-anchoring.** The slider starts *unset* (the group must drag it); the 2019 value
   is only a tick on the axis; and négaWatt's value is never at an end stop nor dead
   centre of the range.
8. **Numbers from the notebook, prose from a hand-edited file.** Same split the card site
   already uses. Change an assumption, re-run the notebook, the workshop follows.

## 3. The levers (inland mobility)

Only genuine **model degrees of freedom** are exposed, so that the reveal is exact and no
group can enter a self-inconsistent scenario. Reference and target values are computed by
the notebook — the table below is the acceptance test, not the source.

| id | Question | Unit | 2019 | nW 2050 | Notebook variable (§) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ground-km-day` | How far will a Belgian travel by motorised transport each day? | km/person/day | 32.68 | 30.48 | `pro_PM_spe` (§1.2.1) |
| `car-share` | What share of those kilometres will still be by car? | % of motorised km | 78.80 | 55.49 | `pro_PM_spe_car` (§2.1.2) |
| `car-occupancy` | How many people in the average car? | persons/car | 1.22 | 2.00 | `occu_trgt_PM_car` (§2.3.5) |
| `car-energy` | Energy per km of the average car | % of 2019 | 100 | 75 | `redu_fuel_PM_car` (§2.3.5) |
| `bike-km-day` | How far by bike per person per day? | km/person/day | 1.68 | 3.27 | `sft_PM_rel_car_to_byc` (§2.1.4) |
| `freight-tkm` | Tonne-km of goods moved per person per year | tkm/person/year | 7 014 | 6 312 | `pro_FT_spe` (§1.2.2) |
| `truck-share` | What share of tonne-km will still go by road? | % of tonne-km | 66.77 | 50.42 | `pro_FT_spe_trk_hvy` (§3.1.1) |
| `truck-load` | Average payload of a truck on the road | tonnes | 12.65 | 13.29 | `pyld_trgt_FT_trk_hvy` (§3.3.3) |

Spare levers, exported but not played in the proof of concept: `bus-occupancy` and
`train-occupancy` (both 100 → 120 % of 2019), plus the destination split of the shifted
car-km (`sft_PM_rel_car_to_*`), which needs an allocation widget rather than a slider.

**`car-occupancy` is the headline lever.** The review calls it *"the single most impactful
and least supported assumption in the model"*: flat at 1.2–1.5 across the EU for 30 years,
and worth 8.55 TWh of swing across its slider range — 37% of the whole 2050
inland-mobility demand, from a single behavioural assumption.

**Leverage, measured.** Swing across each slider's full range, from
`website/data/levers_transport.js` (2050 inland-mobility demand = 23.08 TWh):

| lever | swing | share of the 2050 total |
|---|---|---|
| `ground-km-day` | 10.41 TWh | 45% |
| `freight-tkm` | 8.87 TWh | 38% |
| `car-occupancy` | 8.55 TWh | 37% |
| `car-energy` | 6.02 TWh | 26% |
| `bike-km-day` | 2.80 TWh | 12% |
| `car-share` | 2.67 TWh | 12% |
| `truck-load` | 2.04 TWh | 9% |
| `truck-share` | 0.52 TWh | 2% |

The ranking is partly an artefact of how wide each slider is, so it is not a sensitivity
analysis — but the spread is real, and discovering it is a large part of the point.

**A second deliberate surprise, in `truck-share`.** Shifting road freight to rail and
waterways barely saves energy in this scenario: by 2050 inland navigation needs
0.203 kWh/tkm against 0.111 for an electrified heavy truck, so only the rail share (0.030)
actually saves. Groups reliably expect the opposite.

**A deliberate discussion trap in `ground-km-day`.** The headline −10% mobility reduction
is mostly delivered by aviation: motorised ground travel only falls 6.7% (32.7 → 30.5
km/day), while air travel drops 40%. Most groups expect a far deeper cut to daily travel.
The reveal on this lever is intentionally counter-intuitive and makes a good opener.

## 4. Architecture

Everything is static except the vote API. All definition data ships as `window.*` globals
(the pattern `website/data/*.js` already uses), so the participant page still opens from
`file://` for a dry run.

```
website/
  workshop/{index,play,reveal,facilitator,cards}.html
  workshop/content/<topic>.yaml        HAND-EDITED  (FR/NL/EN wording, facts, justifications)
  assets/css/workshop.css              reuses the :root variables of styles.css
  assets/js/workshop/*.js              spark, i18n, api, impact, levers, play, reveal, …
  data/levers_transport.js             AUTO-GENERATED  (transport notebook)
  data/history_transport.js            AUTO-GENERATED  (data_aux notebook)
  data/workshop_content.js             GENERATED from the YAML
  api/*.php                            PHP 8.3 + PDO/MySQL, same docroot
scripts/
  build_workshop_content.py            YAML → workshop_content.js (+ validation)
  dev_api.py                           stdlib SQLite shim of the same API
  test_workshop_api.py                 end-to-end contract test, --base <url>
  setup-negawatt-workshop-db.sh         the ONE root step
```

### 4.1 Lever data contract

`make_lever()` / `write_levers_js()` in `nW_BE_demand_model_sub_functions.py`, siblings of
the existing `make_hypothesis()` / `write_hypotheses_js()` (whose signature is closed).

```python
make_lever("car-occupancy", topic="inland-mobility",
           name="Car occupancy", unit="persons/car",
           ref_value=1.22, target_value=2.00,
           slider={"min": 1.0, "max": 2.5, "step": 0.05},
           impact={"kind": "inverse", "mode": "car", "twh2050": 8.9},
           model={"var": "occu_trgt_PM_car", "cell": 77})
```

produces `window.NW_LEVERS["transport"] = {generated, title, levers: {...}, model: {...}}`.

`impact.kind` is one of `proportional | inverse | linear-shift | negligible`, evaluated
client-side by `impact.js` against the exported per-mode 2050 TWh and energy intensities.

### 4.2 Backend

MySQL database `negawatt_ws`, four tables: `sessions`, `groups`, `answers` (current state,
unique per group × lever) and `answer_log` (append-only trace of every move). Endpoints:
`session.php`, `group.php`, `answer.php`, `results.php`, `reveal.php`, `selftest.php`.

`results.php` accepts either `?code=` (one session) or `?topic=` (**all open sessions on a
topic** — this is what makes a fully remote workshop work). The reveal page polls it every
3 seconds.

### 4.3 Deployment

`negawatt.squoilin.eu` already serves `/home/negawatt/public_html` over HTTP and HTTPS,
with `AllowOverride All`, PHP 8.3 via mod_php and PDO/mysqli enabled. Consequently: no
Apache change, no systemd unit, no reverse proxy, no certificate work. Publishing is an
`rsync` as the unprivileged `negawatt` user.

Target is the **domain root** (`rsync` *without* `--delete`, so the existing `intervec/`
and `RFNBO_final_results/` folders survive). The workshop is **unlisted** — no nav link,
reachable only by URL or QR code.

The single root step is creating the MySQL database and user; see
`scripts/setup-negawatt-workshop-db.sh`.

## 5. Decisions

Each decision records *why*, so it can be revisited on purpose rather than by accident.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **No Plotly in the workshop module**; a ~120-line inline-SVG helper (`spark.js`) instead | Participants load `play.html` on phones over workshop wifi and `plotly.min.js` is 4.4 MB. Only two chart shapes are needed (historical line, reveal dot plot). Inline SVG also prints crisply on the paper cards. The public card pages keep Plotly. |
| D2 | Levers are the model's **degrees of freedom**, not its outputs | Asking for car share *and* bike share separately would let groups enter inconsistent scenarios, and the reveal could not be exact. |
| D3 | Numbers from the notebook, prose from YAML; the build script **fails loudly** if the id sets disagree | Mirrors the existing card site. Prevents silent drift between the model and the workshop wording. |
| D4 | Slider starts **unset**, négaWatt's value never at an end stop | A default value anchors the group; a range that ends exactly on négaWatt's value gives the answer away. |
| D5 | Leverage is **single-lever** and labelled as such in the UI | Composing all eight needs the interaction terms. Deferred to M8. |
| D6 | **PHP + PDO**, not FastAPI | The user chose the existing MySQL. PHP is the house pattern on this host (`smartcampus`) and needs no root, no systemd, no proxy, no certificate. A Python service would require root edits to both `apache2.conf` and `apache2-le-ssl.conf`. |
| D7 | `dev_api.py` is a **supported artefact**, not only a test fixture | It doubles as a laptop-local server for a workshop with no internet, and it lets the whole UI be built and tested before the DB exists. |
| D8 | Content authored EN first, then FR, then NL | EN is the reference (site and notebooks are EN). **The Dutch needs a native-speaker review before a real workshop** — see open question Q5. |
| D9 | Trilingual via `?lang=` and a `{fr,nl,en}` object per string; the public site stays EN and unchanged | Keeps i18n contained to the workshop module. |
| D10 | Definition data as `window.*` globals, not `fetch`ed JSON | Preserves the site's offline / `file://` friendliness. Only session values need the network. |
| D11 | Workshop **unlisted** (no nav entry) | Stops passers-by joining live sessions and skewing results. Adding a nav link later is a one-line change in `nav.js`. |
| D12 | Deploy to the **domain root**, `rsync` without `--delete` | The docroot has no `index.html` today, so the card site can become the domain landing page; the two existing report folders must survive. |
| D13 | The passenger levers are defined on **motorised ground travel** (road + rail), not on all ground travel | JRC-IDEES has no walking or cycling data, so a curve including them would not exist. Defining the lever on the JRC basis makes the slider *exactly* the endpoint of a real measured series (32.679 observed vs 32.678 in the model). Cycling keeps its own lever, so nothing is lost. |
| D14 | `car-share` and `truck-share` ask for a **share**, not for "% shifted" | "30% of car-km shifted" starts at 0 by construction and has no historical curve. A share does, and the mapping back to the model input is exact because the modal shift is share-preserving on the total. |
| D15 | A lever may point at a **hand-curated** history series supplied by the topic YAML | No car-occupancy time series exists anywhere in the repo (Q6). Rather than drop the curve for the headline lever, the YAML may supply one, clearly marked with its source. The build script checks that every `history` key resolves either in the generated file or in the YAML. |
| D16 | Fixed the pre-existing `_split_powertrains` bug rather than working around it | See §8. The workshop's own energy figures come out of the same helper, so a local workaround would have left a known-broken export in place. |
| D17 | **Nothing shown before a group answers may state négaWatt's own choice**, and the build enforces it | Half the fact cards originally said things like "négaWatt assumes only +5%". That hands over the answer. The notebook now declares, per lever, which exported numbers are *the scenario's choice* (`spoilers=[…]`); `build_workshop_content.py` refuses to put those in a question, subtitle, tangible or ordinary fact, refuses text that prints the target value, and refuses prose that mentions "négaWatt" or "the scenario". Facts that *do* describe the scenario are flagged `reveal: true` and appear only on the reveal screen. |
| D18 | The live leverage readout compares to **2019**, never to négaWatt | Showing "1.1 TWh below négaWatt" while the group is still deciding lets them slide until the difference reaches zero and read the answer straight off the screen. Comparing to the observed 2019 demand keeps the whole point of the readout — feeling how much a lever moves — with nothing to solve for. |
| D19 | A trend fact must be on the **same basis as the curve** the participant sees | The first draft quoted Eurostat's car share (80.7%) next to a chart drawing the model's (78.8%). Both were correct and sourced; together they were confusing. Observed series and their fact now share one basis, and cross-country comparisons live in the benchmark fact, where the change of basis is stated. |
| D20 | Generated files are served `no-cache`; only vendored assets cache hard | Re-running a notebook changes `website/data/*.js`. A facilitator refreshing content between sessions must not leave participants on yesterday's data. `scripts/dev_static.py` does the same locally, which is why it exists. |
| D21 | Printed cards use `min-height`, not a fixed height | Measured at print width, six of the eight cards needed more than the fixed 133 mm and were being silently cut off at the bottom, losing a fact. Cards now start at 128 mm and grow; `break-inside: avoid` keeps each one whole and lets the browser pack the pages. All four pages still hold two cards. |
| D22 | Chart labels follow the page language | `spark.js` reads `<html lang>` rather than hard-coding `en-US`, so a chart next to French prose no longer reads "2.00" where the text says "2,00". |
| D23 | **A workshop is joined from a plain link, not a typed code** | Asking a participant for a code and a group name is two barriers before the first question, and pointless for a remote session. A session may carry a human-readable `slug`, and `…/workshop/play.html?w=namur-2026` joins automatically: the server hands out the next free "Groupe 3" and the page opens on question 1. The four-character code survives as the fallback for reading off a projector, and the facilitator console now shows the link first and the code second. |
| D24 | A group joined from a link can rename itself in one tap | Server-assigned ordinals are fine remotely; in a room the group wants to be "Table 3", and a named group makes the reveal's quotes far more useful. |
| D25 | Joining a different workshop clears the answers held on the device | Found in testing: a device that had answered one workshop and then opened a second link resumed mid-way with the old answers *and* the autosave pushed them into the new group. |
| D26 | `ws_answer_log` deliberately has no foreign key | It is an append-only trace, so deleting a session leaves the record of what was moved and when. Nothing personal is stored. Clean it explicitly if you want a session gone entirely. |
| D27 | **The questions ask for an objective, not a prediction** | The first draft read "In 2050, how many people will be sitting in the average car?", which asks a group to forecast — the wrong act entirely for a normative scenario, and an invitation to answer with what they expect rather than what they think is right. The questions now take one of two shapes: *"What car occupancy is a realistic 2050 objective?"* for the levers that must rise, and *"How far can sufficiency bring daily motorised travel down by 2050?"* for those that must fall. French uses **sobriété** for sufficiency, with *demande sobre* where it reads better. |
| D28 | French typography uses no-break spaces before `?` and `!` and inside guillemets | Not decoration: a plain space let the line break there, and the question headline rendered with an orphaned "?" on its own line. This also forced a fix in the build — `" ".join(text.split())` silently destroys U+00A0, because Python counts it as whitespace, so the folding now collapses ASCII whitespace only. |

## 8. A pre-existing bug found on the way (fixed)

While wiring the workshop's energy figures I found that **`data/energy_totals_overrides.csv`
could no longer be reproduced by re-running the notebooks**, which is the workflow the
README documents. Re-running the transport notebook rewrote the file with `0.0` for
`total road`, `total international aviation` and `total domestic navigation`, and with
`electricity rail` at 0.21 instead of 1.605 TWh.

**This predates the workshop work.** Verified by executing an untouched copy of the
notebook and of `nW_BE_demand_model_sub_functions.py` taken from `HEAD` in a clean
directory: it produces the same zeros.

**Root cause.** `df_PM_TWh_all` / `df_FT_TWh_all` double as display tables: the notebooks
label only the first row of each mode block and leave the `Mode` column blank underneath,
including on the `TOTAL` row. `_split_powertrains()` groups by `Mode`, so every mode after
the first collapsed into one blank-named group and the per-mode totals came out as zeros.

**Fix.** Carry the mode label down (`ffill`) before grouping, in
`_split_powertrains()` — one place, shared by the energy-totals export and by the new
`mode_totals_twh()` the workshop uses.

**Why this is safe.** With the fix, re-running the notebook reproduces **all 57 data rows
of the committed CSV exactly**. The only remaining difference is the `#` comment header,
which already alternates depending on whether the buildings or the transport notebook ran
last — pre-existing churn, unrelated.

**Worth checking on your side:** the PyPSA-Eur fork's `scripts/check_nW_BE_consistency.py`
compares `nW_BE.py` against this CSV. Because the committed CSV happened to hold the
*correct* values, that check was passing — but anyone who followed the README's "refresh
the data files" step would have silently replaced it with zeros and broken the comparison.

## 10. A PHP-only bug the shim could not have had

The first contact with the real database returned an empty `500` from
`POST /api/session.php`. The cause, in the original `session.php`:

```php
$mode = in_array($body['mode'] ?? 'group', ['group', 'solo'], true)
    ? $body['mode'] : 'group';        // <- the ?? guards the test, not the value
```

A request without `mode` passed the check on the *defaulted* value and then inserted
`$body['mode']`, which is `null`. MySQL rejected it, PHP threw, and Apache returned a blank
body. The SQLite shim never had it because there the same line reads `body.get("mode", "group")`.

This is exactly why one contract suite runs against both implementations: it found the bug
on the first run against the deployed PHP, before any workshop did.

Two things changed as a result:

- the value is read once and then validated, rather than defaulted inside a condition;
- `db.php` installs a `set_exception_handler` and a `set_error_handler`, so an uncaught
  error now returns a JSON `500` and a logged detail instead of a blank body — and a
  warning like "undefined array key" is promoted to an exception rather than quietly
  becoming a null.
