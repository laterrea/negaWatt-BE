## Unit Consumption of Services per m²

For thermal uses in the services sector, the indicator is the unit consumption (toe) per m² (buildings surface area), though a unit consumption per m2 of floor area would be more relevant but not enough countries can provide data on the floor area, so energy savings in services are calculated from the variation of unit consumption per employee.

## Climate Corrections

Energy efficiency indicators are climate corrected so that their variation from one year to the other is independent of climatic influences (such as a colder winter implying a higher consumption of heating).

Climatic corrections are done in a linear way on the space heating or cooling consumption on the basis of the ratio between normal degree days and actual degree days, with K being the share of heating consumption independent of climate fluctuations (e.g. 10%) and Hn the consumption at normal climate.

The ODYSSEE-MURE database provides these standardized indicators to track energy efficiency progress across European countries and sectors.

## Overview
The EnSikuMaV is the "Verordnung zur Sicherung der Energieversorgung über kurzfristig wirksame Maßnahmen" from August 26, 2022, with amendments from September 29, 2022.

## 19 Degree Requirement for Public Buildings
Public buildings were previously heated to a maximum of 20 degrees, but under this regulation are now heated only to 19 degrees. More specifically, the regulation specifies 19 degrees Celsius for work requiring light physical activity and predominantly sedentary work (körperlich leichte und überwiegend sitzende Tätigkeit).

## Regulations for Public Non-Residential Buildings
For public non-residential buildings, measures include prohibition of heating common areas that do not serve for occupancy, maximum air temperature values in workrooms distinguished by type and severity of activity between 12 and 19 degrees Celsius, hot water heating systems, and lighting of buildings.

## Timeline
The regulation came into force on September 1, 2022 and was set to expire on February 28, 2023. However, the Bundesrat approved amendments extending the regulation on February 10, 2023.
